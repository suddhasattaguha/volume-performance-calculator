Designing for volume

Where design choices make a difference, and where they stop.

Every interface is designed one at a time, on platforms that already exist. This session looks at one practical question: given those platforms, how much volume can a design absorb, and when does the answer stop being a design question at all?

Part 1 · The real question

Precision is not the point

Inside a range

10 vs 1,000 messages an hour. 5 KB vs 20 KB.

The template holds. Nothing changes.

Across a line

20,000 an hour at global rollout. 2 MB orders. 90 seconds of orchestration each.

The design flips.

Fixed-template platforms have no per-interface autoscaling. The useful work is finding the ranges and the lines between them.

Inside a range, a more precise number changes nothing. Only crossing into the next range changes the design.

New · How volume is counted

The world is not one country times N

One region, office hours

≈ 15–20%

of the day lands in the busiest hour

Three regions, shifted to UTC and summed

≈ 5–12%

of the global day in the busiest hour, depending on the mix

Busiest hour = the highest point of the summed curves, not one country's peak × countries. Central jobs that run at one time add up fully.

Each region's daily curve is shifted by its UTC offset and weighted by how much it contributes; the busiest UTC hour is taken. Three equal regions give about 4–5% of the global day in the busiest hour; a Europe-heavy mix gives 9–12%. Scheduled jobs released centrally break this spreading and must be counted in full.

Each layer gets a zone for the interface's band. The weakest layer decides.

Fragile is the zone most often missed: each change is fine alone, but together they break. Not suitable is not a capacity failure: the component is built for a different traffic shape.

Part 4 · Making it repeatable

The bridge from business answers to bands

Business answers

Users, countries, busiest period, message content, complexity, SLA

The bridge

Fixed formulas, daily curves and a settings table of typical values

Technical envelope

Bands, scale tags, platform verdicts, growth triggers

Busiest hour

peak of the region curves summed in UTC, scaled by country weights

Size

envelope + fields × bytes per field (by format), for normal and biggest

In process at once

messages per second × seconds per message (Little's law)

Backlog clears in

backlog ÷ (capacity − average rate)

The same answers always give the same bands, so the verdict is repeatable across architects and projects. Defaults come from a settings table that architects override when they know better.

Growth becomes a trigger

Biggest message above 10 MB

Check streaming, Solace class, claim check

Busiest hour above 50k

Aggregate, buffer, or move the decision up

Sync need above 225 s

Switch to async request-reply

Years to breach = ln(limit ÷ today) ÷ ln(1 + growth). Example: biggest message 8 MB, Solace cap 30 MB, 40% a year → about 3.9 years.

The layer with the least headroom breaks first.

A design can be right today and fragile in three years. The framework records a trigger: the point where the design must be revisited.
